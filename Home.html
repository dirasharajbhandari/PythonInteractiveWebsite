The Six Kingdoms of Life

🦋

1. Animalia

Group: Animals

Multicellular organisms that obtain food by consuming other organisms.

Examples: Dogs, birds, fish, humans

Animal cells do not have cell walls. Most animals can move during at least one stage of their life.
🌳

2. Plantae

Group: Plants

Most plants make their own food through photosynthesis.

Examples: Trees, grass, mosses, ferns

Plant cells have cellulose cell walls. Most plants contain chloroplasts that help them perform photosynthesis.
🍄

3. Fungi

Group: Fungi

Organisms that absorb nutrients from their surroundings.

Examples: Mushrooms, yeast, mould

Fungi have cell walls containing chitin. Many fungi decompose dead material and recycle nutrients in ecosystems.
🧫

4. Protista

Group: Protists

A diverse group of mostly single-celled eukaryotes.

Examples: Amoeba, Paramecium, Euglena

Some protists make their own food, while others obtain food from other organisms. This is a traditional classification group.
🦠

5. Eubacteria

Group: Common bacteria

Single-celled organisms without a membrane-bound nucleus.

Examples: E. coli, Lactobacillus

Bacteria occur in many environments. Some are beneficial, while others can cause diseases.
🌋

6. Archaea

Group: Archaea

Single-celled prokaryotes distinct from bacteria.

Examples: Methanogens, salt-loving archaea

Some archaea live in extreme environments, while others live in ordinary habitats. Their cell chemistry differs from bacteria.

How Are Organisms Classified?

🔬 Classification Process

1. Kingdom: A broad group of organisms.

2. Phylum: Groups organisms within a kingdom.

3. Class: Divides a phylum into smaller groups.

4. Order: Divides a class into smaller groups.

5. Family: Groups related genera.

6. Genus: Groups closely related species.

7. Species: A basic classification unit.

Example: Human Classification

Kingdom: Animalia
Phylum: Chordata
Class: Mammalia
Order: Primates
Family: Hominidae
Genus: Homo
Species: Homo sapiens

Note: Modern biology also uses three domains: Bacteria, Archaea, and Eukarya.
